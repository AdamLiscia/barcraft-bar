Questions

Questions hosts ask

Do I need a liquor license for my party?

No. You buy the alcohol, I pour it — that's called a dry hire, and it's the standard structure for private events. Nothing is sold at your party, so no license is involved.

What do I need to buy?

Only the alcohol — and you don't even have to work out what. Once we settle the menu I'll call your local liquor store and place the order for you: exact bottles, exact quantities, sized to your headcount. You pay them directly, and most shops around here will deliver it to the house. If you'd rather shop yourself, I'll send you the list instead.

Everything else I can bring as a flat package — juices, sodas and tonics, all the garnish fruit, and the picks, cups, straws and napkins. The syrups and bitters I make myself and bring at no charge either way.

What's in the mixer & garnish package?

Everything that isn't alcohol — $200 for up to 50 guests:

  • Juices — lemon, lime, orange, grapefruit and cranberry. I use Natalie's, which is genuinely good not-from-concentrate juice. Never sour mix.
  • Club soda, tonic and ginger beer
  • All the garnish fruit, cut the day of — plus cherries, olives and fresh herbs
  • Cocktail picks, cups, straws and napkins

The only thing it doesn't cover is ice, which is heavy, cheap, and easier for you to grab locally — it's on your list with an exact quantity.

What does fresh-squeezed citrus add?

For $150 on top, I buy lemons and limes by the case and press them the morning of your party rather than pouring from a bottle.

Bottled juice is fine — the Natalie's in the standard package is what a lot of good bars pour. But fresh is a real step up in a drink like a margarita or a daiquiri where citrus is the cocktail. What you're actually paying for is about three hours of squeezing.

How does the batched drop-off work?

You buy the spirits — same as any event, and I'll place that order for you if you like. They come to me, I turn them into finished cocktails with the juice, syrups, and the right dilution, and I deliver them bottled and ready to pour over ice.

It's $175 plus $3 a drink, 50 drinks minimum, and the swing-top bottles are included and yours to keep. Fifty cocktails works out around $325 — roughly half what the same drinks would cost you at a bar, and nobody has to work your party.

Made with Natalie's juice as standard. Want them hand-squeezed instead? That's $100 on top.

Does setup come out of my four hours?

No. The four hours is bar service — the clock starts when the bar opens and stops when it closes. Setup and breakdown are on my time.

In practice I arrive 60–90 minutes before your start time to set the bar, cut garnishes, and get everything cold, and I stay afterwards to break down and clean up. You're paying for the hours your guests are being served, not for me carrying crates.

How many guests can you handle on your own?

Comfortably up to 50, and I do it regularly. Past that, batching does more of the work and I'll usually recommend a second bartender so nobody stands in a line — it's built into the pricing above.

How far ahead should I book?

Four to six weeks is comfortable. December, New Year's Eve, and late-spring Saturdays book earliest. A 30% deposit holds your date.

What if my guests just want wine and beer?

That's a fine party, and honestly you may not need me. If there are one or two cocktails you want done properly alongside the wine, that's exactly the job — say so on the form and I'll quote it smaller.

Are you insured?

Yes — I carry event liability insurance for every event I work. Once we lock in your date, I'll send you a certificate of insurance, and I can name your venue as an additional insured if they need it.
